Every Question I Get, Answered Honestly

The real questions authors ask before working with me — with honest answers. If yours isn't here, just ask.

General

I help indie authors get their books found on Amazon and beyond. My core work is Amazon discoverability — optimising categories, keywords, and metadata so readers can actually find your book. I also handle launch strategy, PR outreach, content feedback, and distribution planning depending on the engagement.

Most book marketing focuses on the visible layer — social media, email lists, ads. I focus on the invisible layer first: the metadata, categories, and keywords that determine whether readers can find your book at all. Without that foundation, everything else is less effective. I'm also fully async — no calls required — and I only take 1–3 clients per month so every engagement gets my direct attention.

Results & Expectations

No — and I'm suspicious of anyone who does. I don't promise sales, reviews, or bestseller rankings. I promise strategy grounded in a decade of experience. If your Amazon data is set up correctly, sales become possible. But nobody honest can guarantee outcomes.

Because sales depend on many things I can't control — the quality of your book, current market conditions, reader appetite for your genre, timing, luck. What I can control is whether your book is properly positioned to be discovered. That's the leverage point. What happens after depends on the book itself.

Category changes can show impact within 1–2 weeks. Keyword changes typically take 30–60 days to fully reflect. Launch strategies compound over months. Anyone promising overnight results doesn't understand how Amazon actually works.

I'll tell you honestly. If your book has issues that no amount of metadata work can fix — weak writing, poor editing, AI-generated content that reads poorly — I'll let you know upfront. Discoverability makes findable books more visible. It doesn't rescue unfixable ones.

Depends on the pattern. A few 1-star reviews among many is normal. A pile of 1-star reviews all mentioning the same issues (poor editing, AI-generated feel, misleading blurb) suggests the book itself needs work before marketing can help. I'll assess honestly during the audit.

Is This For Me?

Yes. Debut authors often benefit the most from getting discoverability right from day one, before any bad habits or wrong choices get baked in.

Absolutely. Many of my clients are established indies who've been publishing for years but never had the technical layer done properly. There's almost always leverage available in optimising a backlist.

I don't work with unpolished AI-generated books that read badly. Marketing can't fix content problems. If AI was used as a drafting tool but the book is heavily revised and reads well, that's a different conversation.

Then I'm probably not the right person. Covers matter, but they're one variable of many. Authors who overinvest in covers while ignoring metadata typically get poor results. If you're ready to think about your book as a whole system, I can help.
