Children Fiction, Action And Adventure

★★★★★
Children Fiction

The Starting Point And The Problem

Author K.C. had built a genuinely fun, well written children chapter book series aimed directly at reluctant readers, specifically boys aged seven to ten. The book had heart, it had a great voice, and it featured humour and video gaming references that the target readers would absolutely love.

The problem was the disconnect between the product and the platform. Children fiction has a unique discoverability hurdle on Amazon. The people reading the book are kids, but the people typing words into the Amazon search bar are parents and grandparents. If your metadata only speaks to kids, parents will not find it. If it only speaks to parents, the kids will reject the cover and description.

When I audited the listing, I found that the categories had been set at launch and never revisited. The book was sitting in massive, broad categories like General Children Books where it was competing against Harry Potter and Percy Jackson. The keywords were generic single words like adventure, funny, and games. The listing was completely invisible to the exact parents who were desperate to find a book their sons would actually read.

The Audit Findings

  • Wrong BISAC codes were placing the book in overly saturated, highly competitive categories.
  • Keyword slots were wasted on single words instead of long tail buyer phrases.
  • The blurb focused too much on the plot, and not enough on the benefit to the parent.
  • The series page was not linked correctly, causing a massive drop off in read through from book one to book two.

The Strategy And Implementation

We completely rebuilt the foundation of the book. First, we handled the category structure for both the eBook and paperback editions. We moved the book out of the broad categories and requested placement in highly specific, lower competition niche categories like Children Interactive Adventures and Children Video Game Fiction.

Next, we overhauled the seven backend keyword slots. Instead of using single words, we built long tail phrases based on actual parent search behaviour. We used phrases like books for 8 year old boys reluctant readers, funny chapter books for gamers, and action books for 4th grade boys. These are the exact phrases frustrated parents type into Amazon at eight o'clock at night.

Finally, we restructured the blurb. The top line became a hook directed at parents, promising a book that would get their kids off screens and reading. The body of the blurb was written for the kids, focusing on the jokes, the gaming themes, and the fast paced action.

The Outcome

By aligning the metadata with parent search behaviour, the book finally started appearing organically in search results. Because the categories were tightly focused, the book began climbing the niche bestseller lists, which gave it an algorithmic boost. When Author K.C. turned Amazon Ads back on, the cost per click dropped significantly because the ads were finally highly relevant to the search terms.

"Working with Francesca completely changed how I think about my book presence on Amazon. I used to focus on covers and social media. She showed me the invisible side of publishing that actually drives sales. Categories, keywords, backend metadata. Things I had no idea existed. My discoverability transformed."

Author K.C., Children Fiction

Science Fiction, Military Sci Fi

★★★★★
Science Fiction

The Starting Point And The Problem

Author James is a prolific, hard working science fiction writer with a long series that was performing okay, but had hit a hard ceiling. As an experienced indie author, he already understood the basics of Amazon publishing. He knew he needed keywords. He knew categories mattered. He was already spending money running Amazon Ads.

But his ad spend was creeping up, and his return on investment was dropping. He was paying for clicks, but those clicks were not turning into sales or Kindle Unlimited page reads. When an author is getting clicks but no sales, it almost always points to a mismatch between what the ad promises and what the listing delivers.

The Audit Findings

  • The categories were slightly misaligned. The book was sitting in Space Opera, but the content and cover heavily signalled Military Sci Fi.
  • His Amazon Ads were targeting massive, broad sci fi authors. He was paying to put his book in front of Star Wars fans, when he needed to be in front of fans of gritty, tactical marine combat.
  • The A Plus Content was visually messy and did not clearly communicate the reading order of his massive series.

The Strategy And Implementation

Sci fi readers are highly specific. A reader looking for Military Sci Fi wants tactical space battles, squad dynamics, and gritty realism. A reader looking for Space Opera wants sweeping politics, romance, and grand adventure. James book was Military Sci Fi, but Amazon was confused.

We pulled the book out of the broader sci fi categories and pushed it heavily into Military and Fleet combat categories. We stripped out his entire Amazon Ad account and rebuilt it. Instead of targeting broad sci fi terms, we ran highly targeted Product Display ads directly against the specific, niche authors who wrote the exact same flavour of military combat.

We also rebuilt his A Plus Content. We created a clean, visual timeline showing readers exactly what order to read the series in, removing any friction for a reader who wanted to binge the whole universe.

The Outcome

By narrowing the focus, the conversion rate jumped. The Amazon Ads became much cheaper to run because Amazon recognized that when Military Sci Fi readers clicked the ad, they actually bought the book. The algorithmic relevance score improved, which led to Amazon organically recommending the book in the Also Bought sections of major competitors. Less money spent on ads, higher return on investment, and a massive increase in Kindle Unlimited page reads across the whole series.

"Francesca is the real deal. She restructured my categories, rebuilt my keywords, and set up ads that actually converted. She promises strategy, not miracles, and that honesty is exactly why her strategy works."

Author James, Science Fiction

Contemporary Romance, Small Town

★★★★★
Romance

The Starting Point And The Problem

Author A.M. had a completed four book contemporary romance series. The books were well reviewed and beautifully written, but they had been published three years ago. The launch was over. The algorithm had moved on. Sales had dropped to zero. She felt like she had to write a brand new book just to get noticed again.

Romance is the biggest, hungriest, and most competitive market on Amazon. But it is also a market entirely driven by tropes. Readers do not search for a good romance book. They search for grumpy sunshine small town romance or billionaire forced proximity romance. If your listing does not explicitly state your tropes, romance readers scroll right past.

The Audit Findings

  • The blurbs were written like literary fiction. They described the emotional journey of the characters, but completely failed to mention the specific romance tropes.
  • The backend keywords were outdated and did not reflect current romance search trends.
  • The covers were good, but the metadata was not backing up the visual promise of the covers.

The Strategy And Implementation

We executed a complete backlist revival without needing to change the covers or the manuscripts. We started with the blurbs. We completely rewrote the description for book one. We added a bold, punchy headline that immediately established the stakes. We added a bulleted list at the bottom of the blurb that explicitly listed the tropes. Enemies to lovers. One bed. Small town setting.

We updated the backend keywords to match current TikTok and Bookstagram terminology, ensuring that readers searching for those specific dynamics would find her book. We adjusted the categories to target highly specific niche romance lists rather than the general Contemporary Romance bloodbath.

Finally, we set up a targeted Kindle Countdown Deal on book one to feed fresh sales velocity into the newly optimised listing.

The Outcome

The updated blurb and keywords allowed the book to index for terms it had previously been blind to. When the promotion ran, the conversion rate was drastically higher because romance readers saw exactly what tropes were promised. The increased conversion rate pushed the book up the rankings, and the series saw its best read through rate in three years. We revived a dead series purely through technical optimization.

"I finally understand why my book was invisible. I thought my backlist was dead forever. The audit alone was worth it. Clear, direct, and no fluff. We fixed the metadata, and readers actually started finding my older books again."

Author A.M., Contemporary Romance
