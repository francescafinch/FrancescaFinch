K.C.

Author K.C.

Children Fiction, Action And Adventure

★★★★★
Children Fiction

The Situation

Author K.C. had built a fun children chapter book series aimed at reluctant readers, particularly boys aged 7 to 10. The book had heart, voice, humour, and gaming references the target readers would love.

But the book was struggling to reach that audience on Amazon. Categories were set at launch and never revisited. Keywords were generic. The listing was not optimised for how parents and kids actually browse.

What We Did

  • Rebuilt category structure for eBook and paperback
  • Researched keywords for reluctant readers and parent search behaviour
  • Restructured metadata around humour, adventure, and gaming themes
  • Improved series page for stronger read through
  • Refined the blurb for parents and young readers

The Approach

In children fiction, parents buy and kids read. Metadata has to speak to both. We used parent search language and kid relevant themes so the listing worked for both audiences without confusing either.

"Working with Francesca completely changed how I think about my book presence on Amazon. Categories, keywords, backend metadata. Things I had no idea existed. My discoverability transformed."

Author K.C., Children Fiction

Author James

Science Fiction, Military Sci Fi

★★★★★
Science Fiction

The Situation

Author James is a prolific science fiction writer with a series that was performing but not reaching full potential. He understood Amazon basics and was already running ads.

Still, categories were not tightly aligned to military sci fi browsing behaviour. Keywords needed precision. Ads were active but conversion was weaker than the book quality deserved.

What We Did

  • Restructured categories around military sci fi and space opera
  • Rebuilt keyword strategy for sub genre search patterns
  • Restructured Amazon Ads for stronger conversion
  • Optimised series page for read through
  • Refined backend metadata and A Plus Content

The Approach

Sci fi readers are highly specific. Military sci fi, space opera, hard sci fi, and LitRPG all behave differently. We positioned the series clearly so both readers and Amazon algorithm could recognise exactly where it belonged.

A Note On Results

Why I Do Not Share Fabricated Numbers

A lot of book marketers post dramatic before and after claims with no genre, no context, and no verification. I do not do that.

Results depend on the book itself. Strategy cannot rescue weak content, and strong books can move fast once visibility is fixed.

Genres perform differently. Success in cozy mystery does not look like success in literary fiction.

Clients own their data. I only publish what is approved, and I default to initials for privacy.

Trust is built through honesty. I would rather earn confidence than impress with numbers you cannot verify.
