Behind The Work

What Actually Happens When We Work Together

This is what I mean by the invisible work of book discovery. Here is a small window into the kinds of things I look at, dig through, and rebuild for every author I work with.

Deep Keyword Research

Every keyword in every backend slot gets tested against real search data from your genre. I use Publisher Rocket, KDSPY, manual competitor analysis, and Amazon own search suggestion data to build a keyword list that actually reflects how readers search for books like yours.

Category Tree Mapping

I map every possible category your book could sit in, then evaluate each one for competition level, ranking potential, and reader relevance. Then I request placement through Amazon official channels to get you into the exact categories where your readers browse.

Competitor Deep Dives

The top twenty ranking books in your sub genre get analysed for their metadata, positioning, cover conventions, blurb structure, and pricing strategy. Every pattern that emerges tells us something about what your readers respond to.

Metadata Reconstruction

Every backend field on your KDP listing gets reviewed and rebuilt. Title, subtitle, series information, contributors, description, categories, keywords, age range if applicable. Every field matters and most books have at least three or four fields that are actively hurting them.

Amazon Ads Setup

Ad campaigns are built from scratch around your rebuilt keyword strategy. Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brand, and Sponsored Display campaigns work together. Every campaign gets monitored, tested, and refined based on real conversion data.

Ongoing Performance Tracking

Once changes are live, I monitor category rankings, keyword positions, sales velocity, ad performance, and read through rates. Weekly reports keep you informed. When patterns emerge, we adjust fast. Nothing gets set and forgotten.

From The Field

Things I Believe About Indie Publishing

After ten plus years of doing this work, I have opinions. These are some of them.

01

Most authors overinvest in covers and underinvest in metadata

Your cover matters, of course it does. But if your categories and keywords are wrong, the most beautiful cover in the world will not save you. Nobody is seeing your cover in the first place.

02

Amazon Ads without proper metadata is a money bonfire

Paying to drive traffic to a poorly optimised listing is one of the fastest ways to lose money in indie publishing. Fix the listing first. Run ads second.

03

Social media is not a substitute for discoverability

You can post on TikTok every day for a year and still have a book that nobody can find on Amazon. Social media supplements discoverability. It does not replace it.

04

Launch windows matter more than most authors realise

Amazon algorithm pays disproportionate attention to your first thirty to sixty days. A weak launch buries a book that might otherwise have thrived. A strong launch creates momentum that lasts.

05

Going wide is a strategy, not a default

Going wide can be brilliant for the right author. It can also be a distraction from focused KDP Select strategy. The decision should be based on data, not fear or fashion.

06

Reviews follow visibility, not the other way around

Authors obsess about getting reviews and often miss that reviews are downstream of visibility. If nobody sees your book, nobody reviews it. Fix visibility first and reviews start showing up.
