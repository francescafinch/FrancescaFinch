Genres

Multi Genre Expertise, Genre Specific Strategy

Ten plus years working across every major fiction and non fiction genre. Every genre has its own discoverability quirks, reader behaviours, and category politics. Here is how I approach the ones I work with most, and why genre specific strategy always beats generic marketing advice.

Why Genre Matters

There Is No Universal Book Marketing

Cozy mystery readers browse differently than epic fantasy readers. Romance readers search using tropes. Sci fi readers search using sub genres. Children book buyers, who are parents, look for entirely different signals than adult fiction buyers.

Any book marketer who gives you the same advice regardless of genre is not paying attention to how the market actually works.

Every genre below is one I have worked in extensively. Every one has its own strategy playbook. Its own categories that convert. Its own keywords that work. Its own launch tactics that suit it. Its own reader behaviours that shape everything from cover design to blurb structure to pricing strategy.

If your genre is not listed, ask. I have almost certainly worked in it.

Genres I Work In

All Genres, All Formats

From ebook to paperback to audiobook. From chapter books to literary fiction. Every genre has a strategy playbook.

Mystery And Thriller

Cozy mystery, psychological thriller, police procedural, domestic suspense, amateur sleuth, hardboiled crime.

Category politics are especially competitive here. Sub genre precision matters more than almost any other genre. The difference between cozy mystery and traditional mystery matters enormously to readers.

Romance

Contemporary romance, historical romance, paranormal romance, romantasy, romantic suspense, small town, sports romance.

Trope driven searches dominate. Keywords need to match reader shorthand like enemies to lovers, grumpy sunshine, forced proximity, second chance. Miss the trope and miss the reader.

Science Fiction

Space opera, military sci fi, cyberpunk, hard sci fi, dystopian, first contact, LitRPG, alien invasion.

Sub genre expectations are extremely specific. Getting the category wrong loses readers instantly. A hard sci fi reader will not tolerate space opera pacing and vice versa.

Fantasy

Epic fantasy, urban fantasy, cozy fantasy, grimdark, sword and sorcery, portal fantasy, romantasy, LitRPG fantasy.

Series structure is everything. Read through optimization matters as much as first book discoverability. Fantasy readers commit to universes, not just single books.

Children Fiction

Picture books, early readers, chapter books, middle grade, reluctant reader series, illustrated fiction.

Parents are the buyers, kids are the readers. Metadata has to speak to both audiences simultaneously without confusing either one. The best children books nail both.

Young Adult

YA contemporary, YA fantasy, YA romance, YA thriller, YA sci fi, YA dystopian.

TikTok influence is massive in YA. Discovery patterns skew toward viral moments and community driven recommendations. Traditional metadata still matters, but discovery paths differ.

Horror And Paranormal

Supernatural horror, psychological horror, paranormal, gothic, cosmic horror, haunted house.

Sub genre precision matters. Horror alone is too broad, readers hunt within specific niches. Psychological horror readers want different things than supernatural horror readers.

Literary And Contemporary Fiction

Literary fiction, book club fiction, upmarket women fiction, family sagas, cultural fiction.

Categories are less competitive but harder to discover. Positioning against comp titles is critical. Word of mouth matters more here than in genre fiction.

Historical Fiction

Historical romance, WWII fiction, ancient history, biographical historical fiction, Regency fiction.

Time period keywords are gold. Readers search by era with remarkable specificity. WWII fiction, Regency romance, Victorian mystery, all have their own dedicated reader bases.

Non Fiction

Memoir, self help, business, health, personal finance, spirituality, cookbooks, gardening.

Problem solving keywords dominate. Readers are searching for outcomes, not topics. They want to solve a specific problem or learn a specific skill, and your metadata needs to reflect that.

Memoir And Biography

Personal memoir, family memoir, travel memoir, celebrity biography, historical biography.

Hook driven category selection. The angle matters more than the topic itself. What is the specific promise your memoir is making to readers? That drives everything.

The Approach

What I Analyse For Every Genre

Regardless of your genre, the analysis follows the same rigorous framework, adapted specifically to your genre readers and their behaviours.

Search Behaviour

How do readers in your genre actually search? Do they use tropes, sub genres, author comparisons, themes, or something else entirely different?

Category Landscape

Which Amazon categories are worth competing in? Which are so saturated they are a waste? Which niche categories are underused gold mines?

Comp Title Strategy

Which top performing books in your genre are the right comparisons? What can we learn from their metadata, positioning, and blurb structure?

Keyword Patterns

What keywords are searched most in your genre? Which are competitive but achievable? Which long tail keywords have low competition and high intent?

Cover Conventions

Does your cover signal the right genre at thumbnail size? Are you following conventions correctly or breaking them accidentally in ways that lose readers?

Blurb Structure

What blurb structures convert in your genre? Romance blurbs read differently than thriller blurbs, which read differently than literary blurbs.

Launch Rhythm

Some genres favour rapid release schedules. Others reward slower, more considered launches. Your genre determines the tempo that will work best.

Reader Community

Where does your genre reader community live? Facebook groups? Discord? Bookstagram? BookTok? Different genres, different rooms, different rules.
